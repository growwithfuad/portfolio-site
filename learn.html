What clinic websites keep teaching me

Short notes from studying dental clinic ads and landing pages in public — the same observations I share in my LinkedIn series. No clinic names, no claims. Just the patterns.

The notes

Each note is one observation, kept small on purpose. If one of them sounds like your clinic, that's what the free mini-audit is for.

Note 01 · Pricing

The price question

Before anything else, patients ask one question: what does it cost? Some clinic pages answer it right there — a clear price or a clear range. Others hide it behind "call us to find out."

Hiding the price doesn't stop the question. The patient just asks it on a competitor's page instead — and books there. The click is only half the story; the answer on the page is the other half.

Note 02 · Timing

An implant click at 11pm

An implant click at 11pm costs the same as one at 11am. But at 11pm, nobody is at the front desk. The click is counted, the enquiry form waits until morning — and the patient who was comparing three clinics has already heard back from one of them.

Ads don't have to run all night. The schedule is part of the offer, and it should match the hours a real person can continue the conversation.

Note 03 · Diagnosis

The three breaks

When a dental campaign underperforms, I look in three places before I ever look at the budget. The search — is the click coming from someone who wants this treatment, or just a similar-sounding one? The landing page — does it continue what the ad said? The promise — does the offer sound trustworthy, or does it sound like a guarantee?

Most of the time, the break is in one of those three places. Not in the budget.

Note 04 · Message match

Finish each other's sentences

The ad and the landing page should finish each other's sentences. If the ad says "implants from a clear, stated price" and the page opens with a general welcome and a stock photo, the patient has to do the connecting work themselves — and most won't.

Same treatment, same words, same offer, in the same order. When the page continues the ad's sentence, the enquiry feels like the natural next line.

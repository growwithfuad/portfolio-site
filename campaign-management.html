Campaign Setup & Management

Search campaigns for implant & cosmetic dental clinics, built around one idea: every click should come from someone actually looking for the treatment you offer.

Start with a Conversation

The problem this answers

A dental click is expensive. When the campaign structure is loose, the budget quietly goes to people who were never going to book.

One ad group for everything

Implants, check-ups and emergencies in the same group means one generic ad for every intent — and generic ads convert like it.

Broad keywords, wrong patients

Without match-type discipline and a negative list, you pay for job seekers, students and DIY searches alongside real patients.

Ads running while you sleep

An implant click at 11pm costs the same as one at 11am — but at 11pm, nobody is at the front desk to continue the conversation.

How campaigns are built

Structure first, budget second. The same build order, every time — researched from your clinic, your pages and your competitors before a single keyword is chosen.

Separate ad groups for separate intent

Implants, All-on-4, veneers and Invisalign each get their own ad group, their own ads, and their own landing page — because a patient searching for each one is asking a different question.

Keyword & match-type discipline

Keywords are chosen in two stages — the treatment terms first, then the location and price variations patients actually type — with a reason recorded for each one. Exact and phrase match carry the core; anything broader is fenced in.

  • Every keyword has a stated intent and a reason to exist
  • A negative keyword list from day one, not after the waste
  • Local English spellings, the way patients in your area type them

Targeting that respects the front desk

Location targeting set to people in your area — not people who once searched for it — and ad scheduling aligned with the hours your team can actually answer the phone.

Ads and pages that finish each other's sentences

Each ad group's message is matched to its landing page, with AHPRA-aware wording throughout: clear prices and offers, no language that sounds like a promise.

Reporting, honestly

You will always know what the data shows — and what it doesn't show yet.

What was spent, where it went

Search terms, clicks and costs reported plainly — including the searches that were blocked and why.

Observation vs hypothesis

What the numbers prove is kept separate from what I suspect. Changes are made on the first, tested against the second.

No promised patient numbers

I won't forecast enquiries I can't verify. Implant patients decide slowly; cosmetic patients decide faster — the reporting respects both rhythms instead of hiding them.

It starts with research, not a contract

Before any campaign work, I research your clinic's pages and share one real observation. If it's useful, we talk about the audit. Everything else follows from there.
